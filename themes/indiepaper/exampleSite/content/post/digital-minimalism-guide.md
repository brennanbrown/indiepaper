I deleted 500 apps from my phone last year. My life didn't get worse. It got better.

## What is Digital Minimalism?

Digital minimalism is the practice of being intentional about your digital life. It's not about rejecting technology—it's about using technology to support your values, not distract from them.

> *"Minimalism is not about having less. It's about making room for more of what matters."* — Joshua Becker

## The Problem

The average person spends:
- **3 hours** per day on their phone
- **7 hours** per day on screens
- **2 hours** per day switching between apps
- **23 times** per hour checking notifications

That's 12 hours of screen time. Every single day.

## The Audit: Know Your Numbers

Before you can reduce, you need to know what you have.

### Week 1: Track Everything
Install a screen time tracker. Don't judge, just observe.

```bash
# iPhone users
Settings → Screen Time

# Android users
Digital Wellbeing → Digital Balance

# Desktop users
RescueTime or Freedom
```

### Week 2: Categorize Your Usage

Create three categories:

#### Essential
- Communication with loved ones
- Work requirements
- Navigation and utilities
- Creative tools

#### Useful
- Entertainment
- Learning
- Social connection
- News and information

#### Time-wasting
- Mindless scrolling
- Comparison shopping
- Notifications
- Habitual checking

## The Method: 30-Day Digital Declutter

### Week 1: The Phone Cleanse

#### Day 1-3: Delete Obvious Time-wasters
- Social media apps you don't use
- Games you don't play
- Shopping apps you don't need
- News apps that make you anxious

#### Day 4-7: Reduce Useful Apps
- Keep only 1 app per category
- Delete duplicates
- Remove apps with overlapping functions

#### Day 8-14: Question Essential Apps
- Does this app add value to my life?
- Could I do this differently?
- Is there a better alternative?

### Week 2: The Notification Audit

#### Step 1: Turn Off All Notifications
- Phone: Settings → Notifications → Off
- Computer: System Preferences → Notifications → Off
- Email: Turn off all desktop notifications

#### Step 2: Selective Re-enable
- Only allow notifications from:
  - Direct messages from people
  - Calendar events
  - Critical work communications

#### Step 3: Set Check Times
- Check email at 9 AM, 1 PM, 5 PM
- Check messages at 12 PM, 6 PM
- No other notification checking

### Week 3: The Desktop Cleanup

#### Remove Distractions
- Uninstall social media apps
- Use browser extensions sparingly
- Set up focus workspaces
- Hide the dock/taskbar

#### Create Focus Tools
```bash
# macOS
# Create focus workspace
Mission Control → Add Desktop → Name it "Focus"

# Block distracting websites
# Use SelfControl or Cold Turkey
```

### Week 4: The Mindset Shift

#### New Habits
- Single-task instead of multitask
- Batch similar activities
- Use "do not disturb" liberally
- Practice boredom without reaching for phone

#### Digital Boundaries
- No phones at meals
- No screens in bedroom
- No phones during conversations
- No work after 7 PM

## Common Challenges and Solutions

### Challenge: FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)
**Solution**: Realize you're missing out on things that don't matter.

### Challenge: Productivity Guilt
**Solution**: Redefine productivity as meaningful output, not busywork.

### Challenge: Social Pressure
**Solution**: Explain your approach to close friends. Most will understand.

### Challenge: Practical Necessity
**Solution**: Use technology intentionally, not reactively.

## The Philosophy

Digital minimalism isn't about deprivation. It's about intention.

### Questions to Ask Yourself
- Does this technology add value to my life?
- Does it align with my values?
- Does it support my goals?
- Could I achieve the same result differently?

### The Four Questions
1. **Is it essential?**
2. **Is it useful?**
3. **Is it joyful?**
4. **Is it replaceable?**
