I used to be terrified of cooking without a recipe. What if I mess it up? What if it doesn't taste good? What if I waste food?

Then I learned that recipes are training wheels, not cages.

## The Philosophy

Cooking without recipes isn't about being reckless—it's about understanding. When you know *why* you're doing something, you don't need someone to tell you *what* to do.

### The Three Pillars

1. **Technique over Instructions** - Know how to sauté, not just what to sauté
2. **Ingredients over Measurements** - Understand flavors, not just quantities
3. **Intuition over Rules** - Trust your senses, not just the clock

## Building Your Foundation

### Start with Basic Techniques

Master these five techniques and you can cook almost anything:

#### 1. Sautéing
- Heat pan until water evaporates
- Add oil until it shimmers
- Cook until golden brown
- Don't overcrowd the pan

#### 2. Roasting
- High heat (400-450°F / 200-230°C)
- Even spacing on the pan
- Flip halfway through
- Use internal temperature for meat

#### 3. Braising
- Sear first for flavor
- Add liquid halfway up the food
- Low and slow (300°F / 150°C)
- Cover to trap moisture

#### 4. Steaming
- Boiling water below
- Food above, not touching water
- Tight-fitting lid
- Cook until tender

#### 5. Grilling
- Hot grates, clean grates
- Oil the food, not the grates
- High heat for searing
- Let it rest before serving

### Understanding Flavor Profiles

Every cuisine has a flavor profile. Learn these, and you can improvise:

#### Italian
- Base: Olive oil, garlic, onions
- Herbs: Basil, oregano, rosemary
- Acid: Tomatoes, wine, balsamic
- Finish: Parmesan, parsley

#### Mexican
- Base: Lard, onions, garlic
- Spices: Cumin, chili, oregano
- Acid: Lime, tomatoes, vinegar
- Finish: Cilantro, cotija

#### Thai
- Base: Coconut oil, lemongrass, galangal
- Spices: Thai basil, fish sauce, chiles
- Acid: Lime, tamarind
- Finish: Cilantro, peanuts

#### Indian
- Base: Ghee, mustard seeds, cumin
- Spices: Turmeric, coriander, garam masala
- Acid: Yogurt, tomatoes, lemon
- Finish: Cilantro, ginger

## The Method

### Step 1: Assess Your Ingredients

Look in your fridge. What do you have?

```
Today's inventory:
- Chicken breasts
- Bell peppers (red, green)
- Onion
- Garlic
- Rice
- Soy sauce
- Ginger
- Green onions
```

### Step 2: Choose Your Technique

Chicken breasts? Let's do a stir-fry.

### Step 3: Build Your Flavor Base

```
Heat oil → Aromatics (garlic, ginger) → Protein → Vegetables → Sauce → Garnish
```

### Step 4: Taste and Adjust

This is the crucial step. Taste at every stage. Does it need salt? Acid? Heat? Sweet?

## Common Mistakes and How to Fix Them

### Mistake: Under-seasoning
**Fix**: Season at every stage. Salt layers build flavor.

### Mistake: Overcrowding the pan
**Fix**: Cook in batches or use a bigger pan.

### Mistake: Not tasting until the end
**Fix**: Taste and adjust as you go.

### Mistake: Following recipes exactly
**Fix**: Use recipes as inspiration, not rules.

## The Mental Game

### Fear of Failure
Remember: You can always fix it.
- Too salty? Add potatoes or rice
- Too bitter? Add sugar or acid
- Too dry? Add sauce or broth
- Too bland? Add salt, acid, or heat

### The Learning Loop
1. Try something
2. Taste it
3. Note what works
4. Do it again
5. Get better
